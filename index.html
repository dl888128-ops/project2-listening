01 · Authentic Listening Material

The effects of social media on teens’ mental health — American Black Journal (Detroit PBS, October 11, 2023), 6 min 45 sec.

Watch the original PBS report

Use the official PBS player for authentic audio and closed captions.

Open PBS video ↗

Open the video in a second tab. For the first listening, keep captions and the transcript hidden. You can pause or replay the video for later tasks.

Listening goal: Identify the report's central message, recognize specific evidence and advice, and infer why different speakers view social media differently. This report discusses anxiety, depression, and other mental-health concerns; students may choose hypothetical rather than personal examples.

02 · Pre-listening: Activate & Predict

Before watching, think about the topic. There are no right or wrong answers.

A. Which statement best matches your prediction?

B. What information would help teenagers decide how to use social media responsibly?

03 · Top-down Listening: The Big Picture

First listen: Watch the full report without captions. Focus on meaning, not every word.

1. What is the report's main purpose?

2. Which sequence best describes the report?

04 · Bottom-up Listening: Hear the Details

Second listen: Replay relevant sections. Listen for specific wording, numbers, and speaker recommendations. Turn on captions only after answering.

3. Why did one student say she deleted TikTok?

4. What daily-use threshold does the medical expert mention when describing a research association?

5. What does one expert especially advise parents to do?

05 · Inferential Listening: Read Between the Lines

Third listen: Pay attention to the speakers' attitudes and the implications of their words.

6. Why does one expert mention both connection and a downside?

7. What does the report's final question imply?

06 · Explicit Language Focus: Giving Advice

TARGET LINGUISTIC FEATURES

In the report, speakers discuss how adults can support teenagers. Notice how English speakers give advice with different degrees of directness.

Form, meaning & use

Imperative: Listen to your children. — A direct recommendation. The subject you is understood.

should + base verb: Parents should listen carefully. — A recommendation, often less direct than an imperative.

It's important to + base verb: It's important to question what you see online. — Emphasizes a principle or priority.

The first example is a brief excerpt from the PBS transcript. The should and It's important to sentences are instructional examples, not quotations from the video.

Guided practice

8. Choose the grammatically correct advice.

9. Which sentence uses an imperative to give advice?

07 · Post-listening: Digital Well-being Campaign

COMMUNICATIVE OUTPUT TASK

Situation: Your school is preparing a digital well-being campaign for teenagers. You are a student adviser. Create a short campaign message that helps students make informed choices about social media.

Your deliverable: Write 80–120 words in English. Include (1) one concern or benefit from the PBS report, (2) two practical recommendations, (3) a reason for each recommendation, and (4) at least one target advice structure.

Self-check before sharing




Optional communication extension: Share your campaign with a partner and revise it after feedback. For individual study, read it aloud as a 45-second announcement.

08 · Self-study: Practice Independently

Review the video with captions after your first attempts. Then answer these transfer questions.

10. Which advice best fits the report's overall conclusion?

11. Complete the recommendation: “It's important ___ different perspectives online.”

Reflection

Which listening strategy helped you most: predicting, listening for key details, or inferring meaning? Explain why.

09 · Source, Transcript & Teacher Notes

Original source: Detroit PBS, American Black Journal, “The effects of social media on teens’ mental health,” October 11, 2023 (6:45).

PBS original video and full official transcript ↗

Transcript: On the PBS page, select Transcript below the player. The full transcript remains hosted by PBS; it is not reproduced here. For course submission, include the official transcript link and attach the transcript separately if the instructor requires a standalone script.

Teaching rationale: Pre-listening activates prior knowledge. Top-down questions target gist and discourse organization. Bottom-up questions direct attention to specific information. Inferential questions require interpretation of speaker stance and the report's conclusion. The explicit TLF lesson supports a communicative campaign task with an identifiable audience and outcome. Independent practice provides immediate corrective feedback.

Teaching note: The report discusses associations between social media use and mental-health outcomes; students should not interpret these associations as definitive proof of causation. Avoid asking students to disclose private mental-health experiences.
